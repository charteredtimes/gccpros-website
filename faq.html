GCC FAQ & Glossary

Plain, practitioner answers on Global Capability Centres, how to set one up in India, and what the jargon actually means.

What is a GCC (Global Capability Centre)?

A Global Capability Centre is an offshore or nearshore unit that a company owns and operates itself to deliver strategic work such as engineering, data and AI, finance, analytics, cyber security and customer operations. Unlike outsourcing to a third party, a GCC keeps the talent, IP and institutional knowledge in-house. It is also called a captive centre or, historically, a Global In-house Centre.

What is the difference between a GCC, a GIC, a captive, a BOT and an ODC?

GCC, GIC and captive all describe the same thing: a company-owned offshore centre. GIC (Global In-house Centre) is the older term that GCC has largely replaced. BOT (Build-Operate-Transfer) is a setup model where a partner builds and runs the centre for a defined period and then transfers it to you. An ODC (Offshore Development Centre) is a narrower, usually engineering-only version of the same idea.

How large is India's GCC ecosystem?

India hosts more than 1,700 Global Capability Centres employing well over 1.5 million professionals, and it remains the world's largest and fastest-growing GCC hub. Centres span banking, technology, pharma, retail, industrials and more, and the mandate has shifted from cost centres to genuine centres of capability and innovation.

Why do global companies set up GCCs in India?

Three reasons compound: a deep, English-fluent talent pool across engineering, finance and analytics; a meaningful cost advantage against onshore locations; and a maturing ecosystem of infrastructure, government support and peer centres to learn from. Increasingly the driver is value creation and access to scarce skills, not cost alone.

Which Indian cities host the most GCCs, and how do we choose one?

Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Delhi NCR (Gurugram and Noida) and Mumbai are the primary hubs, each with a different talent and cost profile. The right city depends on the functions you are building, the seniority of talent you need, real-estate and cost targets, and proximity to peer centres. This is exactly the call our location strategy work is built to get right, using data on thousands of existing centres rather than opinion.

How do you set up a new GCC in India, step by step?

At a high level: define the mandate and target operating model; build the investment business case; select the city and map talent; choose the entity and setup model (own build versus Build-Operate-Transfer); secure incentives and approvals; sign the first leader; stand up infrastructure and hire the founding team; and move to steady-state operations. GCCPROs supports each of these stages as a practitioner-led, vendor-neutral advisor.

How long does it take to stand up a GCC?

A focused greenfield centre typically takes six to twelve months from decision to a live, hiring operation, depending on scale, entity choice and whether you build directly or use a Build-Operate-Transfer partner. Location, entity and first-leader decisions taken early are what compress or stretch that timeline.

What does a GCC cost, and what is cost arbitrage versus value creation?

Costs are driven by headcount, seniority mix, city, real estate and the operating model. Cost arbitrage means running the same work at a lower total cost than onshore; value creation means the centre owns higher-value outcomes such as product engineering, research or transformation. The two are not mutually exclusive, and the right target should be an explicit choice, not an accident.

How do we find the first leader for our GCC?

What government incentives are available for GCCs in India?

Central and state governments offer a range of incentives including land and infrastructure support, single-window clearances, and various fiscal and employment-linked benefits that differ by state and city. GCC glossary

The terms you will hear, in one line each.

GCC
Global Capability Centre. A company-owned offshore or nearshore unit delivering strategic work in-house.
GIC
Global In-house Centre. The earlier term for a GCC.
Captive
Another name for a company-owned offshore centre, as opposed to outsourcing to a third party.
BOT
Build-Operate-Transfer. A partner builds and runs the centre, then transfers ownership to you.
ODC
Offshore Development Centre. A usually engineering-focused version of a captive centre.
Cost arbitrage
Delivering the same work at a lower total cost than onshore locations.
Value creation
Owning higher-value outcomes such as product engineering, research or transformation, beyond cost saving.
Site head
The senior leader accountable for a GCC location; often the most important early hire.
Fractional CXO
A seasoned executive who partners part-time to help stand up or steer the centre during setup.
