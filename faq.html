GCC basics

What is a GCC (Global Capability Centre)?

+

A Global Capability Centre is an offshore or nearshore unit that a company owns and operates itself to deliver strategic work such as engineering, data and AI, finance, analytics, cyber security and customer operations. Unlike outsourcing to a third party, a GCC keeps the talent, IP and institutional knowledge in-house. It is also called a captive centre or, historically, a Global In-house Centre.

What is the difference between a GCC, a GIC, a captive, a BOT and an ODC?

+

GCC, GIC and captive all describe the same thing: a company-owned offshore centre. GIC (Global In-house Centre) is the older term that GCC has largely replaced. BOT (Build-Operate-Transfer) is a setup model where a partner builds and runs the centre for a defined period and then transfers it to you. An ODC (Offshore Development Centre) is a narrower, usually engineering-only version of the same idea.

How large is India’s GCC ecosystem?

+

India is the world’s largest and fastest-growing GCC hub. The GCCPROs database tracks 2,503 Global Capability Centres in India, employing well over 1.5 million professionals across banking, technology, pharma, retail, industrials and more. The mandate has shifted from cost centres to genuine centres of capability and innovation.

Why do global companies set up GCCs in India?

+

Three reasons compound: a deep, English-fluent talent pool across engineering, finance and analytics; a meaningful cost advantage against onshore locations; and a maturing ecosystem of infrastructure, government support and peer centres to learn from. Increasingly the driver is value creation and access to scarce skills, not cost alone.

Setting up in India

Which Indian cities host the most GCCs, and how do we choose one?

+

Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Delhi NCR (Gurugram and Noida) and Mumbai are the primary hubs, each with a different talent and cost profile. The right city depends on the functions you are building, the seniority of talent you need, real-estate and cost targets, and proximity to peer centres. Our location strategy work uses data on thousands of existing centres rather than opinion.

How do you set up a new GCC in India, step by step?

+

Define the mandate and target operating model; build the investment business case; select the city and map talent; choose the entity and setup model (own build versus Build-Operate-Transfer); secure incentives and approvals; sign the first leader; stand up infrastructure and hire the founding team; and move to steady-state operations. GCCPROs supports each stage as a practitioner-led, vendor-neutral advisor.

How long does it take to stand up a GCC?

+

A focused greenfield centre typically takes six to twelve months from decision to a live, hiring operation, depending on scale, entity choice and whether you build directly or use a Build-Operate-Transfer partner. Location, entity and first-leader decisions taken early are what compress or stretch that timeline.

How do we find the first leader for our GCC?

+

The first leader sets the culture, hiring bar and credibility of the centre, so this is the single most important early hire. GCCPROs helps identify candidates through its community of active GCC leaders who have built centres of a similar size, rather than a generic search.

How do we choose a GCC consulting firm?

+

Ask what the advice rests on rather than whose logo is on it: how many centres the team has actually built and run, whether they are neutral on entity and location or are selling you a build-operate-transfer contract, who does the work once the pitch is over, and what evidence sits behind the recommendation. Our guide to choosing a GCC consulting firm sets out the questions worth asking in the first meeting.

Cost, talent & incentives

What does a GCC cost, and what is cost arbitrage versus value creation?

+

Costs are driven by headcount, seniority mix, city, real estate and the operating model. Cost arbitrage means running the same work at a lower total cost than onshore; value creation means the centre owns higher-value outcomes such as product engineering, research or transformation. The two are not mutually exclusive, and the right target should be an explicit choice, not an accident.

What government incentives are available for GCCs in India?

+

Central and state governments offer incentives including land and infrastructure support, single-window clearances, and fiscal and employment-linked benefits that differ by state and city. Because these change and vary by location, we help evaluate and access the right package as part of location strategy, and can facilitate introductions to the relevant policy makers.
